# Friend domains: area contract

Every named friend owns a part of the Overworld. The seven boss domains keep
their hidden titles until the identity reveal. The other eleven are resolved
through an exploration challenge, trade, puzzle or shared task; "conquering"
them does not turn allies into enemies. Each domain needs its own terrain,
architecture, local block family, vegetation, mob or creature, useful loot,
playable rule and public townhall service. These are design targets, not claims
that the areas already exist.

Feel's gothic direction is fixed by the user. Hamed's paper-and-timber fantasy
comic district is the current candidate for the requested Japanese/anime
aesthetic because his verified "Fish Story 2" power-system line gives it a
creative reason to exist. The architecture should draw from real machiya and
tea-house references, then become an original game space rather than a copy of
a specific building. Four friends lack verified direct quotes; their identity
details remain provisional until speaker evidence or user direction arrives.

## Build rules

- First author a landmark and at least two different side-building types per
  domain. Terrain and vegetation must join them; a flat template pad fails.
- A mob family and item should use the same palette and story as the district.
  Palette swaps alone do not make a new area.
- A domain's local rule must be readable in the environment and fair to any
  starting gem, a solo player and a small co-op group.
- Townhall services use the world victory record. Late joiners can use them,
  and all effects have clear durations, costs or recharge times.
- New textures come from inspected assets with explicit redistribution rights.
  Store source files and deterministic transforms, then inspect the output in
  Minecraft. No AI image generation is used.

Hushwire has a first shared architecture, townhall service and naturally
generated Hushwire Heath biome. Hamed has a discoverable three-building
architecture, two placeable materials and naturally generated Inkbound Grove.
His recipe puzzle and public desk are implemented in code, with in-game
interaction, save/restart and co-op validation still open. Ragab has three
connected gaming buildings and six individual casino games. The free daily
tournament can be entered through Ragab, while each of the six game-bay signs
now opens that table's rules and stake directly. Players can finish a round at
the sign without walking back to Ragab; he remains the host and the way back to
the full table list.
The daily tournament uses server-settled versions of those games: three rotating solo
tables plus the optional two-player Signal Table. One player can clear the
three solo tables; friends can split the work. The daily shared score saves
in world data; new once-per-player bread-and-torches claim receipts save in
playerdata with the granted inventory. The first completed day saves Ragab's
domain unlock. An isolated 26.3 dedicated-server run covered visible-clock
rollover, disconnect and repeated restart; natural casino play with external
solo/co-op clients and visual review remain open.
